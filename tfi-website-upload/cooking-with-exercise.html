Cooking with Exercise

Moving, nourishing, and belonging — together.

Picture a Tuesday morning in Rochester. Ten people — some moving slowly, a few with tremors, one pushing a walker — gather in a community kitchen. Two hours later they've stretched, balanced, laughed, and made a pot of turkey-and-vegetable soup from scratch. They leave with a container of soup, a recipe card, and — just as importantly — each other's phone numbers.

That is what Cooking with Exercise looks like. And for people living with Parkinson's disease in our community, it is exactly what's been missing.

Why it matters

One program, three gaps closed at once

Accessible, affordable, PD-specific programming is scarce in our community. Cooking with Exercise was designed to meet three urgent needs together.

Adaptive movement

Most fitness programming isn't designed for people with movement disorders. Generic classes can feel unsafe or embarrassing. Ours is built for tremor, limited grip, and fatigue.

Hands-on nutrition

Eating well gets harder as PD progresses. We teach practical, adaptive cooking skills — meeting people exactly where they are in their own kitchens.

Genuine belonging

Isolation is one of the hardest parts of living with Parkinson's — and it worsens disease progression. We build consistent, judgment-free community.

We started by listening

Designed with the community, not at a desk

We didn't design this program in a boardroom. We designed it in conversation — over the course of a year — with people actually living with Parkinson's disease.

Our partnership with the Rochester Parkinson's Network (RPN), a peer-led organization with deep roots and deep trust in the local PD community, opened the door. Together we held listening sessions and focus groups with RPN members and family caregivers, and fielded a short community survey.

What we heard

Four themes, striking in their consistency

1 · Movement built for them

People want to exercise — but generic classes feel unsafe or embarrassing for those with movement disorders.

2 · Nutrition that's hands-on

No program in Rochester pairs nutrition education with adaptive, hands-on cooking instruction.

3 · An end to isolation

People crave consistent, judgment-free community as much as any physical benefit.

4 · Independence, protected

Above all, the quiet determination to stay independent — and support that actually makes it possible.
